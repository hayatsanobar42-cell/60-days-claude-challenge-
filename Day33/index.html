Concept · Headline Accuracy

Headlines are written to be clicked, not to inform you.

A headline's job is to earn a click — sometimes at the cost of accuracy. Words like "SHOCKING," vague scope ("this common thing"), or urgency ("BREAKING") can make a small, boring fact feel huge and dangerous.

Why it matters

Most people only read the headline, so a misleading one can shape someone's entire belief about an event — even if the article itself is more careful.

Everyday example

A headline says a food is "linked to cancer." The study actually tested rats fed extreme doses for years — nothing like normal human consumption.

    Concept · Emotional Manipulation

    If a post makes you feel something fast, that's the point.

    Anger, fear, and aspiration spread faster than calm, accurate information. Posts engineered for emotion often target a specific audience's insecurities or grievances, using loaded words instead of facts.

    Why it matters

    Content that makes you feel first and think later is more likely to be shared impulsively — which is exactly why manipulative content is written that way.

    Everyday example

    "Everyone is going to notice the difference ✨" isn't information — it's designed to create insecurity so you keep watching or buying.
